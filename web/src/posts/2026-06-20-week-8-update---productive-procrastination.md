## Week 8 Update — Builder Season Playbook

This week felt like an important transition point.

Up until now, most of the work has been focused on running the Piece of Pie hackathon itself. This week I started packaging the systems, templates, documents, and workflows into something that could become an actual product.

In practice, that meant documenting what we're already doing and collecting those materials into a structured folder system. For whatever reason, I ended up using an Obsidian vault as the container. An Obsidian vault is really just a folder structure with some helpful tools around it, so eventually I may replicate the whole thing into a GitHub repository. Right now, the important thing is creating a place where all of the artifacts can live together.

One realization this week is that the Builder Season Playbook is not just a collection of documents. It's a collection of processes, examples, templates, and evidence gathered from actually running a builder season. The challenge is organizing those pieces in a way that someone else can pick up and use.

I also had an opportunity to discuss the concept with a group that may be interested in running their own Builder Season-style hackathon. Those conversations were valuable because they forced me to think less about how the system works internally and more about how it is explained to organizers, sponsors, and potential customers.

My next step is to iterate on the landing page and gather more feedback from people who might actually run a program like this. The landing page is becoming a useful tool because it forces me to clarify the value proposition and explain the outcomes in simple language.

One thing I've been thinking about this week is the psychology of procrastination.

There is still a lot of work to do: documents to create, workflows to define, data models to refine, and product positioning to test. Yet I found myself highly motivated to work on an entirely different side project. I had energy, momentum, and focus — just not on the thing I had planned to work on.

I'm not sure that's laziness. It feels more like a form of productive procrastination. Sometimes the work that matters most carries the most uncertainty. Building the product means making decisions, committing to directions, and exposing unfinished ideas to feedback. Working on a side project can feel easier because the stakes are lower and the path is clearer.

The lesson for me this week is that motivation is not always a reliable indicator of priority. Sometimes the most important work is the work that creates a little resistance.

The goal for next week is simple: continue organizing the playbook, improve the landing page, and create something tangible enough that potential organizers can react to it and tell me what is missing.
