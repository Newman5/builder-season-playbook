## Week 6 Update — Builder Season Playbook

This week has been interesting because I’ve been struggling a bit with turning the Builder Season Playbook into an actual product. I keep thinking about the materials, templates, processes, and systems, but I realized I may need to build the marketing website and value funnel first so that I can clearly see the shape of the thing myself.

Sometimes making the prototype helps define the product.  Like the palm pilot guy with the wooden device he used to show people.

It also gives me something concrete to show other people. A website, landing page, or prototype creates a mental picture that is easier to react to than a repo full of documents and ideas.

One strategy I’ve used before on projects is pretending I only have a short amount of time left to make it ready. It changes the mindset from endless planning into asking: “What would actually matter if I had to ship this soon?” That framing has helped me prioritize before, and I think it’s helping again now.

The example Piece of Pie project continues to go well and has been getting positive reviews and feedback, which has been encouraging.

Speaking of feedback, I did a feedback session with the Trike Mobility team and Nori this week. It was invigorating and inspiring. It reminded me how valuable structured conversations can be, especially when people are actively building.

I also started thinking about utility tools that could support the project management side of the playbook. One idea is an import script for Work Breakdown Structure documents, allowing builders to create a WBS in whatever format or PM tool they prefer and then import it into another system. The portability and interoperability of planning tools feels important.

Another insight this week was recognizing the different types of users of the project. Builders, admins, organizers, contributors, sponsors, reviewers — they all interact with the system differently, and the playbook probably needs to reflect those perspectives.

Before the next feedback session, I want to have more than just a repository. I want some kind of prototype that can collect the artifacts and deliverables already being created. Right now I’m imagining something like a Kanban board combined with an Obsidian vault — a workspace that could eventually be transformed into a website or distributable product.
